> What happens if someone who loves eating can simply stop getting full?

MAD EATER is an AI-made absurd science-fiction serial, published as two-to-four-minute vertical shorts on TikTok and YouTube Shorts.

Season 1 is complete: nine episodes, from a restaurant buffet to a galactic energy program.

A man builds a machine that keeps the nutrition his body needs and transfers the rest somewhere else.

**The machine works.**

He can keep eating.

<mark>That is where the trouble starts.</mark>

## 00 / Eat forever

At first, he only wants to eat more.

He goes to an expensive restaurant and eats through an absurd amount of food.

A billionaire notices the machine.

The offers begin:

**$100 million.**

**$1 billion.**

**$10 billion.**

He still refuses.

Eventually, he starts a club.

Membership is not decided by net worth.

First, you have to prove how much you can eat.

## 01 / The club

The club fills with billionaires, politicians, celebrities, officials, and other people who are very used to getting what they want.

They eat.

And keep eating.

But the food does not simply disappear.

The machine sends it elsewhere.

At first, that looks useful.

Hunger falls.

Then ordinary people start gaining weight without eating.

The club begins influencing how much food the world produces.

Eating becomes part of status.

People train their jaws.

Faces change.

A machine built so one person could have another course of dinner has somehow become everybody else's problem.

## 02 / It keeps getting bigger

People resist.

A war starts.

Both sides eventually discover that eating itself can be used as a weapon.

Then aliens arrive.

They reveal that the machine is using a form of space-transfer technology that never belonged to Earth in the first place.

That should probably be the point where the story gets smaller.

It does not.

## 03 / Making it

MAD EATER is built largely with generative image and video tools, then assembled through editing, voice, sound, and a lot of continuity repair.

The practical limitation is simple: generative video is usually much better at producing a few useful seconds than an entire scene.

So the series is made in pieces.

| The model gives | The edit has to rebuild |
| --- | --- |
| short clips | scenes |
| separate performances | conversations |
| changing locations | continuity |
| fragments | episodes |

A conversation may be generated as separate shots for each character.

A five-second piece of action may need to carry part of a much longer scene.

Concretely: video, voice, and stills are generated with Jimeng's Seedance model, and every generated shot carries a single action lasting four to five seconds. EP08 was planned as 45 numbered shots and EP09 as 76 — compressed into roughly fifty generated clips across the two episodes. UI screens, news captions, and data curves are finished in post instead of being generated.

News broadcasts, surveillance footage, newspapers, public announcements, and fast montage appear constantly because they work both as storytelling devices and as ways to connect material that could never be generated as one continuous take.

The process is less about asking a model to "make a film" and more about finding enough usable pieces to make the film in the edit.

## 04 / Still growing

MAD EATER was not planned from the beginning as a giant universe.

It started with:

one man,

one machine,

and one very small wish:

**keep eating.**

Then every solution created a bigger problem.

A restaurant became a club.

The club became a world system.

The world reached the galaxy.

The galaxy kept going.

Season 1 is complete — nine episodes. The scripts for the next three are already written.

<span class="reverse">The appetite keeps expanding.</span>
